## The story

The d12rg benchmark thread kept turning criticism into tools. v0.4.0 answered the first three reviews; the redundancy layer (shared as v0.4.1 on the list) answered Marcel Wende's question *"how many independent routes converge on each answer, and what survives if one is removed?"*. Marcel's follow-up sharpened it one notch further — and this release answers that. It also formally tags the route-redundancy layer, which until now lived only in the working tree.

## 1. Derivation-route redundancy — independent routes + single points of failure (M. Wende)

The leave-one-route-out dual of the v0.4.0 structural-load ranking. `DerivationDAG` gains:

- `independent_routes(target)` — internally node-disjoint derivation routes from the structural ground to a target (Menger's theorem, via unit-node-capacity max flow). Conjunctive premises that funnel through one closed form count as **one** route; a value reached two genuinely disjoint ways (a commuting identity, or a benchmark moved by several independent integers) counts as **many**. Routes ≥ 2 is the redundancy.
- `cut_nodes(target)` — the single points of failure: nodes whose removal leaves the target underivable from the axioms (a node on only *some* of several routes is not a cut node).
- `route_redundancy()` and `criticality_ranking()` — per-target route count + SPOFs, and nodes ranked by *criticality* (outputs ungrounded if the node is removed) beside *load* (outputs reached), with `redundancy = load − critical`.

```
$ python -m nwt_substrate.benchmarks.o10 --suite --sensitivity --redundancy

  alpha (coupling)   load 19   critical  6   redundancy 13
  DIM_S_SPIN7 (= 8)  load 14   critical  1   redundancy 13
```

The result revises the load ranking's story: **high reach is not high criticality.** `DIM_S_SPIN7` moves the most benchmarks, but α is the *sole* route for six pure-α observables, so α is the more critical node. Adding α as an explicit root lifted resilience from 16 → 21 dual-route benchmarks; 10 benchmarks have no detected structural route at all (exact group-theory outputs, measured-input-anchored), and that's surfaced, not hidden.

## 2. Derivation diversity — route independence beyond route count (M. Wende follow-up)

Marcel's next point: route *multiplicity* is not route *independence*. Two routes can be node-disjoint in the DAG yet collapse under the same perturbation — *"three routes that all fail under the same perturbation may be less robust than two routes from substantially different structural sectors."* And, he noted, the information to measure this already lives in the sweep.

It does. Each integer's **mover-set** (which benchmarks shift when it is perturbed ±1) is its empirical perturbation-response signature. Integers with the same signature are one structural **sector**, not two routes. `SensitivityReport` gains:

- `integer_similarity(a, b)` — Jaccard overlap of two integers' mover-sets (the empirical "correlated perturbation response", at the binary granularity the sweep records).
- `integer_sectors(threshold)` — single-linkage clustering of the load-bearing integers into sectors by response overlap.
- `route_diversity(threshold)` — per benchmark: `raw` routes (distinct integers) vs `effective` routes (distinct sectors). `effective < raw` is false redundancy; `effective == 1` with `raw ≥ 2` is a **hidden single point of failure**.

```
$ python -m nwt_substrate.benchmarks.o10 --suite --sensitivity --redundancy
  ...
  7 structural sectors among 15 load-bearing integers;
  14 benchmarks lose routes to shared response (5 collapse to a single sector)

  {DIM_ADJ_SPIN7, DIM_INTERNAL_SU2, DIM_V_SPIN7, H_COXETER_SO7, N_EDGES_K7, N_VERTICES_K7}
  {K8_ACTIVE_EDGE_COUNT, K8_SEESAW_EDGE_DIFFERENCE, N_VERTICES_K8}
  {DIM_S_SPIN7, N_LORENTZ_FROM_CL07}

  [hidden SPOF]  bhabha / moller / muon_decay_rate / qed_compton / qed_eemumu   raw 2 -> 1 effective
```

The finding, in Marcel's own terms:

- **Five QED/lepton benchmarks** that the route count called 2-route "resilient" are single-sector: `DIM_S_SPIN7` and `N_LORENTZ_FROM_CL07` always co-move (`N_LORENTZ`'s 8 benchmarks ⊂ `DIM_S`'s 15, Jaccard 0.53). Effective routes: 1. Hidden SPOFs.
- **`DIM_V_SPIN7 ≡ N_EDGES_K7`** are perturbation-identical (Jaccard 1.0) — `21 = 3·7 = C(7,2)` showing up as response-degeneracy, not coincidence. The whole K₇/SO(7)-geometry cluster collapses to one effective route, as does the K₈ seesaw trio.

### Two honest limits

1. **The five hidden SPOFs are threshold-contingent** — they hinge on the single Jaccard-0.53 spine link, and revert to 2-route above threshold 0.53. The geometry- and K8-sector collapses are *threshold-robust* (true up to exact Jaccard-1.0 identity). The robust collapses are the firm result; the QED five are flagged-but-threshold-sensitive.
2. **Only the perturbation-response measure carries signal here.** Of the graph-side measures (critical-node / ISA-ancestry overlap), all degenerate because nearly everything funnels through α, so "shared ancestry" reports α everywhere. In an α-rooted theory, *empirical* route-diversity is the right lens and *topological* route-diversity is the wrong one — itself a small finding.

## What's next

The O10 *ladder* proper (`A:B :: C:D` rung transport across two vertical systems) remains open, pending the Mills GUTCP ↔ NWT/CFT source material. Re-counting effective routes under a *magnitude-weighted* (not binary) perturbation response is the natural next refinement of the diversity layer, if the sweep is extended to record signed shifts.
